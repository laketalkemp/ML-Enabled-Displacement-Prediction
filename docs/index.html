Overview

The global infrastructure for climate risk monitoring exhibits a structural asymmetry that deepens existing inequities: the communities most exposed to climate-driven displacement are, in many cases, the communities least visible to standard climate data products. Sparse ground-station coverage, limited satellite telemetry integration, and spatial resolutions optimized for policy-relevant regional aggregates all contribute to a data environment in which local, site-specific conditions, precisely those conditions that determine whether a household can or cannot remain in place, remain systematically underresolved.

This project addresses that asymmetry directly. The Displacement Hotspot Vulnerability Mapping Tool develops a composite vulnerability index pipeline that synthesizes INFORM Risk Index variables with UNHCR-IDC Vulnerability Screening Tool dimensions, producing tiered vulnerability scores at spatial resolutions sufficient for humanitarian agency planning. Methodologically, the project draws on super-resolution generative adversarial network architectures and spatially-aware clustering to extract site-specific signal from coarse satellite inputs, calibrating outputs against available ground-truth sensor data to correct systematic biases inherent to global reanalysis products in heterogeneous terrain.

Recognition Justice, the principle that communities must be rendered legible and present within the systems that shape decisions about their welfare, and Distributive Justice, the demand that exposure to climate harm and access to protective resources be evaluated in relation to social structures of advantage and disadvantage, together motivate both the selection of vulnerability dimensions and the design of the output scoring tiers. Outputs are structured for direct use by humanitarian agencies operating in data-scarce regions, where the absence of high-resolution climate data compounds the inequities that displacement itself produces.

Methodology

Composite Index Construction

The vulnerability index integrates variables from the INFORM Risk Index framework, including hazard and exposure measures, coping capacity indicators, and population vulnerability dimensions, with the structured screening criteria of the UNHCR-IDC Vulnerability Screening Tool. Variable selection prioritizes indicators available at humanitarian-relevant spatial scales and demonstrably associated with reduced adaptive capacity, drawing on displacement literature to anchor selection decisions in documented mechanisms rather than data availability alone.

Tiered scoring is structured across four thresholds — low, mild, moderate, and severe — calibrated to reflect meaningful distinctions in adaptive capacity. Tier boundaries are informed by field research on displacement thresholds and the operational categorizations used by humanitarian agencies in intake and prioritization workflows.

Spatial Analysis Pipeline

Initial spatial analysis applies unsupervised clustering, including Principal Component Analysis and K-Means, to UNHCR vulnerability variables at regional scale, producing cluster typologies that reveal latent structure in displacement risk concentrations. These cluster outputs anchor the spatial correlation analyses that relate vulnerability index scores to documented displacement patterns and humanitarian agency case data.

The downscaling component applies physics-informed super-resolution generative adversarial networks to ERA5 reanalysis fields in high-priority displacement contexts, incorporating a Thermodynamic Regime Classifier to preserve physical consistency across diverse climate regimes. Bias correction against ground-truth sensor telemetry reduces systematic errors from the global reanalysis calibration domain.

Data Description

Coarse Satellite Inputs

ERA5 — ECMWF Fifth-Generation Atmospheric Reanalysis

Standard climate models operate at resolutions insufficient for localized humanitarian action. ERA5, the fifth-generation atmospheric reanalysis product from the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts, is the de facto global standard for retrospective climate analysis, providing hourly atmospheric and land-surface estimates at approximately 31 km horizontal resolution. While ERA5 represents the most comprehensive global climate record available, its calibration is weighted toward regions with dense observational networks, producing geographic biases that systematically overlook the rural and underserved regions where displacement risk is highest. This project takes ERA5 as its coarse satellite input and applies a machine learning pipeline to produce site-specific climate estimates at resolutions suitable for monitoring communities that existing models render effectively invisible.

Sparse Validation Data

Ground-Truth Field Sensor Telemetry

Communities in regions with sparse ground-level monitoring are effectively invisible to standard climate models, leaving them vulnerable to unpredictable disasters. The ground-truth sensor data used in this project surfaces that invisibility as a technical and ethical challenge rather than a data exclusion criterion. In-situ sensors deployed in target displacement contexts measure surface meteorological conditions, including temperature, humidity, precipitation, and wind, at the site-specific scale relevant to humanitarian planning. Sparse coverage is treated as a documented feature of the equity problem: monitoring gaps are quantified and incorporated into the uncertainty estimation of output vulnerability scores, rather than silently propagated as false precision. Bias calibration of satellite-derived estimates against these sensors corrects the geographic biases that standard models impose on rural and underserved populations.

Vulnerability Framework

INFORM Risk Index

Translating complex climate variables into actionable insights for populations at risk of displacement requires a vulnerability framework grounded in the conditions that determine whether a community can withstand a single extreme heatwave, drought, or flood event, the kind of event that often triggers a cascade of internal displacement, economic instability, and physical insecurity. The INFORM Risk Index, developed by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee Task Team on Humanitarian Data and Preparedness, provides that grounding. Its three primary dimensions, hazard and exposure, socioeconomic vulnerability, and lack of coping capacity, are computed from over fifty sub-indicators drawn from internationally recognized sources. This project draws on the vulnerability and coping capacity sub-indicator set to anchor the composite index in dimensions directly linked to adaptive capacity and displacement risk, ensuring that the outputs inform policy and resource allocation rather than reproduce abstract risk abstractions.

Humanitarian Alignment

UNHCR-IDC Vulnerability Screening Tool

Data science as a tool for human rights and protection is only as effective as its accessibility to the agencies doing protection work. The UNHCR-IDC Vulnerability Screening Tool provides the structural logic that makes this project's outputs immediately usable by humanitarian organizations in the field. The VST's screening dimensions, covering physical protection risks, health and medical vulnerability, gender-based vulnerabilities, and barriers to livelihoods and services, define the categorical framework against which this project's tiered vulnerability scores are calibrated. Alignment with the VST is a deliberate design choice: it ensures that climate-derived signals can be integrated into existing intake and prioritization workflows without requiring agencies to learn a new system. The goal is to extend the benefits of high-resolution environmental monitoring to those most in need of representation, with the VST serving as the bridge between data and decision.

Provider United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) Last Updated Periodically revised; verify current version at time of access URL unhcr.org — Vulnerability Screening Role Tier structure and screening alignment for humanitarian agency compatibility
Socioeconomic Context

World Bank Development Indicators

Climate risk does not operate independently of the social and economic conditions that determine who can adapt and who cannot. World Bank Development Indicators, spanning income, poverty, health outcomes, education, infrastructure access, and governance quality, provide the socioeconomic context necessary to ground vulnerability scores in the material realities of the communities this project aims to protect. These indicators anchor the Principal Component Analysis used to construct spatial cluster typologies, ensuring that groupings of displacement risk reflect measurable dimensions of human welfare rather than geographic proximity alone. Variable selection prioritizes indicators with documented relationships to adaptive capacity, including income per capita, access to improved water and sanitation, under-five mortality, and governance effectiveness, following the weighting approach developed in the preliminary case study.
