Extreme Weather and Environmental Displacement

Extreme weather events, exacerbated by human caused climate change, disproportionately impact communities with limited socioeconomic resources and political representation. For marginalized populations, a single extreme heatwave, drought, or flood event often triggers a cascade of internal displacement, economic instability, and physical insecurity.

Standard global circulation models inadvertently obscure localized extreme weather events, leading to high prediction uncertainty due to coarse geographic resolution. This lack of localized data is a critical bottleneck in anticipating and mitigating these localized crises, delaying a proactive humanitarian response.

Project Objective

To deploy a physics informed deep learning pipeline and spatial correlation models to downscale ERA5 meteorological data, generating highly accurate vulnerability maps for targeted displacement hotspots to empower proactive supply prepositioning.

Methodological Architecture

Translating deep learning predictions into actionable humanitarian intelligence.

Objective Data Weighting via PCA

In traditional social sciences, researchers often build indices by subjectively deciding that certain indicators are worth specific percentages. This framework eliminates human bias. The algorithm mathematically weighted the input matrices based purely on how much they actually contribute to the variance between regions. The composite score is entirely data driven, isolating a clean metric from highly overlapping environmental variables.

Humanitarian Alignment and Clustering

A raw statistical score is not actionable for humanitarian agencies. To bridge this gap, the consolidated data is processed through K Means clustering to organize regions into distinct operational tiers.

  • These tiers Low, Mild, Moderate, and Severe perfectly align with the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees operational protocols.
  • This directly determines eligibility for targeted relief and proactive supply prepositioning.

Addressing Epistemic Erasure

Standard open source geographic mapping libraries often fail to align with global humanitarian datasets due to complex geopolitical naming conventions or disputed borders. Because of these structural data rigidities, highly vulnerable populations frequently fall out of the data matrix entirely.

This architecture purposefully acknowledges these gaps as a demonstration of Recognition Justice. For the Fall technical milestones, the goal is to manually reconcile these specific geographic polygons to ensure administrative boundary disputes do not prevent communities from receiving an accurate vulnerability classification.
